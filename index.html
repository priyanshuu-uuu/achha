I do not know if an apology can travel backwards, find the exact evening where everything felt a little heavier because of me, and quietly change what I should have done differently. I wish it could. I wish I could take every moment in which you needed a hand and I gave you distance instead, every silence where you deserved comfort and received nothing from me, every time you must have wondered why the person who was supposed to understand you suddenly became another reason for you to feel alone. If regret could rebuild things, perhaps I would have finished the whole house by now. Unfortunately, I was gifted with regret instead of magic, which is probably the universe's funniest way of teaching an idiot a lesson. I know I hurt you. Not in some beautiful, poetic way either. I hurt you in the most ordinary and painful way possible: by not being there when being there mattered. I left empty spaces where my presence should have been, and I know no amount of explaining can make those spaces disappear. You should not have had to ask for the kind of care that should have come naturally. You should not have had to wonder whether you mattered to me. And you definitely should not have had to carry certain nights alone while I was somewhere else, living as though time would politely wait for me to grow a brain. It did not. Neither did those moments. There is something else I have understood much later than I should have. Distance never taught my heart another language. I may have been far away, I may have taken time, I may have looked like I was learning how to live without looking back, but somehow every road kept bending toward the same name without ever saying it aloud. I wrote things I never sent. I found myself staring at old traces of you for far longer than any emotionally stable person should admit. I searched for little pieces of your existence from a distance, then pretended I was merely curious. Absolutely convincing performance. Oscar committee, please contact me. But perhaps the funniest proof was that even when I was not beside you, I was still writing toward you. Some people move on by replacing what they had. I apparently chose the much less intelligent method of carrying it quietly and pretending it weighed nothing. And maybe that is why I cannot make myself understand the word “give up.” It sounds simple when spoken by someone standing outside the feeling. For me, it feels like being asked to stop recognising a song simply because it ended too early. I can accept anger. I can accept distance. I can accept that some wounds need time before they trust a hand again. What I cannot promise is that I will suddenly stop loving you just because things became difficult. I do not want to make a dramatic promise that I will never make another mistake, because that would be another lie dressed up as devotion. I am still human, unfortunately. What I can promise is that I will learn from the ones I have already made. I will listen instead of assuming. I will speak instead of disappearing. I will ask when I am unsure. I will stay present when things become uncomfortable instead of choosing silence because it is easier for me. And I want you to know something else, something I hope you never again have to ask for: you will not have to beg me to be there. Not because I want to play some heroic character who saves everything, but because I finally understand that love is often much less dramatic than that. Sometimes it is answering. Sometimes it is checking in. Sometimes it is sitting quietly beside someone without trying to fix them. Sometimes it is staying patient when the other person has every reason to be guarded. I want to become the kind of person whose presence does not need to be requested every time the weather changes. If you need space, I will respect it. If you need silence, I will not punish you for it. If you need me, I will be there. And if you do not have the strength to ask, I will still care enough to notice. I cannot promise you a perfect future, because apparently even my present comes with surprise updates. I cannot promise that there will never be another disagreement, another bad day, another moment where one of us gets tired or confused. But I can promise that I will not treat difficulty as an exit sign. I will choose conversation over disappearance, patience over pride, and effort over convenience. I will keep showing you through actions what these words are trying so desperately to say. Not for a week. Not until things become comfortable again. I mean the slow, boring, ordinary kind of commitment that survives after the poetry is finished. And yes, I love you betuuuu. I think I have been trying to disguise the simplicity of that sentence under a thousand metaphors because saying it plainly somehow makes it more frightening. But there it is and it is what it iz. I love you betuu. I loved you when I was close, I loved you when I was far, I loved you in all those ridiculous moments(created by me only, lawl ) when I should have been doing literally anything else and somehow ended up writing about you again. I loved you through distance badly, imperfectly, sometimes selfishly, and sometimes silently. I want to learn how to love you better now, not merely louder. If life gives us years, I want those years to contain more than grand promises. I want them to contain small proofs. The message sent when you had a terrible day. The hand that stays when things become difficult. The apology that comes without being dragged out. The patience that does not disappear after one uncomfortable conversation. The little efforts nobody applauds. The kind of love that becomes so ordinary that one day you realise you never had to ask whether it was there. Maybe I am asking for another chance with a heart that has already given me enough chances to know better. Maybe I am ridiculously late to my own lesson. Maybe I deserve the anger you still carry. I will not argue with that. I only hope that someday, when the sharpness of everything becomes softer, you will be able to see that the person who once failed to stay is trying, with everything he has, to become someone who knows how. So I am not asking you to forget. I am not asking you to heal faster. I am not asking you to pretend that nothing happened. Let the hurt take its time. Let trust come back slowly. Let me earn what I once treated carelessly. And if the road takes a lifetime, then let it be. I'll be there forever.... I would rather spend that lifetime learning how to reach the same light properly and endlessly than spend one day pretending I never wanted it. Because somewhere along the way, without asking permission and without giving me a sensible exit plan, you became the person my heart kept choosing and will be choosing forever. And apparently, for once in my life, I am not interested in teaching it otherwise. ❤️
